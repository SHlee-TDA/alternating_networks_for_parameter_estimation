\section{Proofs of Theoretical Properties}
\label{app:proofs}

We provide the proofs for the three properties stated in \Secref{subsec:theory}. Throughout, we
argue symmetrically over the two decoders of A-DCVAE---the Hidden-State CVAE decoder $g_\phi$ and
the Parameter CVAE decoder $g_\psi$---and drop the sub/superscripts when a statement holds for both.
We write the generic decoder as $g(\vu, \xi, \vz)$, where $\vu = \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is the fixed
context, $\xi$ is the conditioning variable subject to noise, and $\vz$ is the CVAE latent. We assume
$g$ is $C^2$ in $\xi$ and that expectations over $\vz \sim \mathcal{N}(\vzero, \mI)$ are finite.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:contraction} (Condition noise $\Rightarrow$ local Jacobian regularization)}
\label{app:proof_thm1}

Let the condition be perturbed by $\check{\xi} = \xi + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c$ with
$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c \sim \mathcal{N}(\vzero, \sigma_c^2 \mI)$, while the decoder is trained to
reconstruct the clean target $Y$. A second-order Taylor expansion of the decoder about the clean
condition $\xi$ gives
\begin{equation}
    g(\vu, \xi + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c, \vz)
    = g(\vu, \xi, \vz) + \nabla_{\xi} g\, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c
      + \tfrac{1}{2}\, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c^\top \nabla_{\xi}^2 g\, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c
      + \mathcal{O}(\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c\|^3).
\end{equation}
Substituting into the reconstruction term of the ELBO and taking the expectation over
$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c$, the linear term vanishes because $\E[\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c] = \vzero$,
and the leading quadratic contribution is
\begin{equation}
    \E_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c}\!\left[ \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c^\top (\nabla_{\xi} g)^\top (\nabla_{\xi} g)\, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c \right]
    = \sigma_c^2\, \Tr\!\big( (\nabla_{\xi} g)^\top (\nabla_{\xi} g) \big)
    = \sigma_c^2\, \big\| \nabla_{\xi} g \big\|_F^2 .
\end{equation}
Taking the expectation over the latent prior and using that, for a sufficiently expressive network,
the mean residual $\E[Y - g]$ is small so the Hessian cross-term is higher order, the objective
reduces asymptotically to
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:tikhonov}
    \gL^{\mathrm{noisy}} \;\approx\; \gL^{\mathrm{clean}}
    \;+\; \sigma_c^2\, \E_{\vz}\!\left[ \big\| \nabla_{\xi} g(\vu, \xi, \vz) \big\|_F^2 \right]
    \;+\; \mathcal{O}(\sigma_c^4).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eqn:tikhonov} is exactly a Tikhonov penalty on the expected condition-Jacobian. We
emphasize that this \emph{suppresses} $\|\nabla_{\xi} g\|_F$ rather than certifying a global
Lipschitz bound. Its dynamical consequence is dissipativity: for the composed transition, the
expected one-step change of $\| \nabla g \|_F$ is negative outside a bounded region, so the map acts
as a Lyapunov drift that keeps the chain within a bounded set instead of amplifying perturbations.
Combined with the compact boundary projections $\mathcal{B}_y, \mathcal{B}_p$, this prevents the
exponential error amplification of Issue~1. $\hfill\square$

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:score} (Target noise $\Rightarrow$ denoising score matching)}
\label{app:proof_thm2}

Let the target be perturbed by $\tilde{Y} = Y + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t$ with
$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\vzero, \sigma_t^2 \mI)$. By the calculus of variations, the
reconstructor minimizing the expected squared error is the posterior mean,
\begin{equation}
    g^*(\tilde{Y}) = \E\big[ Y \mid \tilde{Y}, \vu, \vtheta \big]
    = \int Y\, p\big(Y \mid \tilde{Y}, \vu, \vtheta\big)\, dY .
\end{equation}
By Bayes' rule, $p(Y \mid \tilde{Y}) \propto \mathcal{N}(\tilde{Y} \mid Y, \sigma_t^2 \mI)\,
p_{\mathrm{data}}(Y \mid \vu, \vtheta)$. Using the Gaussian identity
$\nabla_{\tilde{Y}} \mathcal{N}(\tilde{Y} \mid Y, \sigma_t^2 \mI)
= -\tfrac{\tilde{Y}-Y}{\sigma_t^2}\, \mathcal{N}(\tilde{Y} \mid Y, \sigma_t^2 \mI)$ to isolate
$Y\,\mathcal{N}$, substituting into the integral, and applying Leibniz's rule yields Tweedie's
formula \citep{efron2011tweedie},
\begin{equation}
    g^*(\tilde{Y}) - \tilde{Y}
    \;=\; \sigma_t^2\, \nabla_{\tilde{Y}} \log p_{\mathrm{data}}\big(\tilde{Y} \mid \vu, \vtheta\big).
\end{equation}
Hence the decoder's residual equals the score of the smoothed conditional density. Injecting noise
at inference and applying $g^*$ therefore realizes one step of a Langevin drift toward high-density
regions of $\mathcal{M}$, i.e.\ an attractor field that resolves Issue~2. $\hfill\square$

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ergodicity} (Ergodicity and score-aligned convergence)}
\label{app:proof_thm3}

Consider the alternating kernel $\mathcal{T}$ of \eqref{eqn:kernel}, obtained by composing the two
infinite Gaussian mixture kernels.

\textbf{(i) Unique invariant measure.} Each sub-kernel integrates an isotropic Gaussian against the
latent prior, so its transition density is strictly positive, $k(x, y) > 0$, on the compact domain
$\Omega$ enforced by $\mathcal{B}_y, \mathcal{B}_p$. Strict positivity implies the chain is
$\varphi$-irreducible, aperiodic, and Harris recurrent. By Theorem~\ref{thm:contraction}, the
Tikhonov-induced dissipativity supplies a Lyapunov drift condition of Meyn--Tweedie type \citep{meyn2009markov}. Together
these give geometric ergodicity: there exist $\rho \in (0,1)$ and $C < \infty$ such that
$W_2\big(\mathcal{T}^k(\mu_0, \cdot),\, \pi^*\big) \le C\, \rho^k$ in the $2$-Wasserstein metric, for
a unique invariant distribution $\pi^*$.

\textbf{(ii) Score-aligned target and no collapse.} Substituting the optimal reconstructor of
Theorem~\ref{thm:score} into the update shows that each step is an Euler--Maruyama discretization of
the unadjusted Langevin dynamics \citep{welling2011bayesian}
\begin{equation}
    \mX_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(k+1)} \leftarrow \mX_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(k)}
    + \alpha\, \nabla \log p_{\mathrm{data}}\big(\mX_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(k)}\big) + \sqrt{2\alpha}\, \boldsymbol{\epsilon},
    \qquad \alpha \propto \sigma_t^2 ,
\end{equation}
whose stationary law (via the Fokker--Planck equation) is the score-defining density. Because a fresh
latent $\vz$ is drawn at every step, $\mathcal{T}$ is an infinite mixture with the capacity to place
mass along an entire multi-modal (e.g.\ crescent-shaped) support rather than at a single mean.
Consequently $\pi^*$ aligns with the true non-identifiable manifold and the chain bypasses the
mode collapse of deterministic solvers. $\hfill\square$

\paragraph{Remark (scope of the guarantees).}
Theorem~\ref{thm:contraction} yields a Jacobian penalty and dissipativity, not a certified global
contraction; the invariant law $\pi^*$ is the stationary distribution of the learned stochastic
operator and approximates $p_{\mathrm{data}}$ up to the score-matching and $\epsilon$-incompatibility
errors of the trained decoders. These are the intended, and empirically supported, senses of the
three guarantees.
