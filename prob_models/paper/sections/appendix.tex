\section{Proof Sketches}
\label{app:proofs}

The full statements, assumptions, and proofs of Theorems~\ref{thm:existence} and \ref{thm:steering}
are developed in the companion manuscript \citep{adcvae-theory}. Here we sketch the three ideas.
Throughout, $\xobs$ is fixed, $\Omega = \Omega_H \times \Omega_\Theta$ is the compact convex state
space produced by the boundary projections $\mathcal{B}_y,\mathcal{B}_p$, and $\mathcal{T}$ is the
alternating kernel of \eqref{eqn:kernel} with $\theta$-marginal one-sweep kernel $K$.

\paragraph{Existence (Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}), via Doeblin minorization.}
Each conditional draw $\mathcal{B}\big(g(\cdot,\vz) + \sigma\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\big)$ has, on the
interior of the compact $\Omega$, a Lebesgue density bounded below by a positive constant: the
additive Gaussian $\sigma\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ (with $\sigma>0$) has full support, and $g$ is
continuous on the compact latent/condition domain. Hence the composed kernel satisfies a Doeblin
minorization $\mathcal{T}(x,\cdot) \ge \varepsilon\,\nu(\cdot)$ for all $x$, which yields a unique
invariant law $\pi^\ast$ and geometric convergence in total variation, uniformly over the initial
state \citep{meyn2009markov}. Both hypotheses (compactness, positive noise) hold by construction, so
no contraction is assumed. A deterministic decoder ($\sigma=0$) gives a Dirac kernel with no such
density lower bound, formalizing why the stochastic sampler is needed.

\paragraph{Rate, via a noise-cancelling coupling.}
Injecting noise into a decoder's \emph{condition} and reconstructing the clean target is
asymptotically a Tikhonov penalty on the condition-Jacobian $\sigma_c^2\,\E\|\nabla_{\mathrm{cond}}
g\|_F^2$ \citep{bishop1995training}, which promotes a condition-Lipschitz constant $L$. Coupling two
copies of a sweep by sharing the latent \emph{and} the injection noise, the shared noise cancels
inside $\mathcal{B}(g(\cdot)+\sigma\boldsymbol{\epsilon}) - \mathcal{B}(g(\cdot')+\sigma
\boldsymbol{\epsilon})$ (projections are $1$-Lipschitz), leaving $\le L\|\cdot-\cdot'\|$. Composing
the two half-sweeps gives a $2$-Wasserstein contraction of $K$ with constant $\kappa = L_H L_P$; when
$\kappa<1$ this yields a geometric mixing rate. The contraction is governed by the condition-Lipschitz
constants, whereas the injection noise sets the \emph{width} of $\pi^\ast$---the two are separate
knobs, which is why tightening mixing does not induce collapse.

\paragraph{Steering (Theorem~\ref{thm:steering}), via fixed-point perturbation.}
Let $K^\dagger$ be the sweep built from the true posterior conditionals; since these are conditionals
of one joint, $K^\dagger$ is exact Gibbs and its $\theta$-marginal invariant is the true posterior
$p(\vtheta\mid\xobs)$. For a $\kappa$-contraction $K$ with invariant $\nu^\ast$ and any $K^\dagger$
with invariant $\nu^\dagger$, the Banach perturbation inequality gives
\[
    W_2(\nu^\ast,\nu^\dagger)\ \le\ \frac{1}{1-\kappa}\,\sup_\theta W_2\big(K(\theta,\cdot),K^\dagger(\theta,\cdot)\big),
\]
and interpolating one conditional at a time bounds the operator discrepancy by
$L_P\varepsilon_H + \varepsilon_P$, giving \eqref{eqn:steering}. The gap is the conditional
approximation error amplified by the mixing constant; it vanishes when the conditionals are exact.
The self-consistency certificate $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{inc}} = W_2(\Pi^\rightarrow,\pi^\ast)$, the
$W_2$ gap between the forward-paired joint $\Pi^\rightarrow(d\vtheta,d\mX_{\mathrm{hid}}) =
\nu^\ast(d\vtheta)\,Q_H(d\mX_{\mathrm{hid}}\mid\vtheta)$ and the stationary joint, is zero iff the
learned conditionals are compatible; it is dominated by $\varepsilon_H,\varepsilon_P$ and, unlike
them, is computable from the trained model alone.
