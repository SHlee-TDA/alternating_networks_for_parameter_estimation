\begin{abstract}
Estimating parameters of nonlinear dynamical systems from sparse and partial observations is a highly ill-posed inverse problem. While structural decoupling of state and parameter estimation can ease non-convex optimization, deterministic iterative networks that enforce global contraction via strict spectral normalization often suffer from severe expressiveness bottlenecks. Consequently, in structurally unidentifiable regimes, deterministic models inevitably lead to model collapse, where predictions fall into the uninformative conditional mean (regression-to-the-mean). To overcome this fundamental limitation, we present a probabilistic framework that lifts the decoupling philosophy into a generative space. We propose a Dual Conditional Variational Autoencoder (Dual CVAE) framework integrated with Pseudo Gibbs Sampling for iterative inference. We mathematically prove that injecting noise into the conditioning variables asymptotically imposes a Frobenius norm penalty on the network's Jacobian, inducing a Tikhonov regularization effect that bounds local Lipschitz constants and guarantees empirical MCMC convergence. Extensive numerical evaluations on biological systems demonstrate that our framework completely bypasses model collapse, successfully capturing structured, multi-modal uncertainty landscapes (bimodal posteriors) along unidentifiable valleys where traditional networks fail.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Inferring the governing parameters of nonlinear dynamical systems from sparse, noisy, and partially observed trajectories is a cornerstone challenge across mathematical biology, ecology, and physiology. The task is fundamentally hard for three compounding reasons: parameter estimation is an ill-posed inverse problem; \emph{sparse} sampling maximizes its ill-conditioning; and \emph{partial} observation induces structural non-identifiability, in which many parameter configurations produce the same observable trajectory.

Existing approaches trade one difficulty for another. Traditional optimizers (nonlinear least squares, adjoint methods, MCMC) navigate highly non-convex landscapes and are prone to local minima or gradient divergence. A single amortized network $\hat{\vtheta} = f(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}})$, in the spirit of simulation-based inference \citep{cranmer2020frontier}, is fast but high-variance, tending to memorize the data rather than learn the underlying dynamics; physics-informed networks \citep{raissi2019physics} re-optimize per instance and are inefficient at inference. Our prior work introduced a \emph{deterministic split} framework that decouples the map $\mX_{\mathrm{obs}} \mapsto \vtheta$ into $\mX_{\mathrm{obs}} \mapsto \mX_{\mathrm{hid}} \mapsto \vtheta$ via two networks and guarantees convergence of the resulting fixed-point iteration through global Lipschitz bounds ($L < 1$) enforced by strict spectral normalization \citep{miyato2018spectral}. Empirically, however, this global contraction severely penalizes representational capacity, and---more critically---in structurally non-identifiable regimes the MSE objective projects all valid latent trajectories onto a single uninformative average. This \textit{model collapse} (regression-to-the-mean) isolates predictions within empty regions of the parameter manifold.

In this work we shift from point-wise deterministic recurrence to probabilistic manifold tracking. Rather than a single point estimate, we approximate the joint posterior, casting non-identifiability into a structured \emph{uncertainty} that is itself the quantity of interest in medical and biological applications. We instantiate the decoupled tasks as two Conditional Variational Autoencoders (Dual CVAE) and, at inference, alternate between them with a stochastic Pseudo-Gibbs routine that samples hidden states $\mX_{\mathrm{hid}}$ and parameters $\vtheta$ given sparse observations $\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}$. Crucially, we replace rigid global contraction with a softer, data-driven regularization: injecting variance-controlled noise into the conditioning variables during training is asymptotically equivalent to a Frobenius-norm penalty on the condition-Jacobian, imposing a \emph{local}, dissipativity-inducing constraint that stabilizes the chain without sacrificing expressiveness.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We introduce a generative, decoupled framework that retains the physical partitioning of hidden states and parameters while enabling expressive uncertainty modeling via a dual-network structure.
    \item We demonstrate that condition-noise injection acts as an implicit Tikhonov regularizer over the network's Jacobian matrix, controlling local Lipschitz bounds and enabling stable empirical MCMC convergence.
    \item We show that our framework successfully uncovers multimodal, structured posteriors (bimodal distributions) along non-identifiable manifolds, completely bypassing the regression-to-the-mean failure mode of deterministic regression.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background \& Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
Consider a nonlinear dynamical system governed by the ordinary differential equation (ODE) $\dot{\vx}(t) = f(\vx(t); \vtheta)$, where $\vx(t) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ represents the full system states and $\vtheta \in \mathbb{R}^p$ denotes the constant parameters. In realistic scenarios, we only observe a subset of the states $\vx_{\mathrm{obs}} \subset \vx$ at highly sparse time intervals, leaving $\vx_{\mathrm{hid}} = \vx \setminus \vx_{\mathrm{obs}}$ latent.

Prior deterministic iterative networks constructed a mapping sequence $\mX_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(k+1)} = H_\phi(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \vtheta^{(k)})$ and $\vtheta^{(k+1)} = P_\psi(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \mX_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(k+1)})$, defining the operator $T(\vtheta; \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}) = P_\psi(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, H_\phi(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \vtheta))$.
By enforcing a global Lipschitz constant via spectral normalization such that $\mathrm{Lip}(T) < 1$, convergence to a unique fixed point $\vtheta^*$ was guaranteed via the Banach Fixed-Point Theorem, and teacher forcing aligned $\vtheta^*$ with the true parameter.
However, as analyzed in subsequent sections, this rigid global restriction severely bottlenecks representation capacity and collapses under multi-modal parameter supports.

\section{Analysis: Expressiveness vs. Robustness Trade-off}
\label{sec:analysis}
To stress-test our model, we evaluate inference degradation by incrementally injecting Gaussian noise into the test observations $\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}$. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:noise}, this experiment uncovers a compelling trade-off between a model's local expressiveness and its vulnerability to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) perturbation.

\begin{figure}[h]
\begin{center}
\fbox{\rule[-.5cm]{0cm}{3.5cm} \rule[-.5cm]{6cm}{0cm}}
\end{center}
\caption{\textbf{Noise Sensitivity Profile:} Deterministic baselines maintain a high-bias, flat-line error response. In contrast, generative architectures exhibit high-variance spiking curves once observation noise pushes inputs off the clean manifold.}

The high sensitivity of the Dual CVAE originates directly from its superior performance: it maps a razor-thin, precise geometric manifold of the physics equations. When observation noise pushes inputs off-manifold, the generative alignment falters, leading to variance spikes. This reveals that structural precision introduces sensitivity to noise, defining a distinct bias-variance frontier.

\section{Conclusion \& Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a probabilistic decoupled framework for nonlinear inverse tasks that replaces global spectral constraints with local Jacobian regularization. By modeling conditional supports over complete physical state spaces, our architecture completely resolves model collapse in unidentifiable regimes, recovering detailed multimodal posteriors. While the sharp expressiveness of our network yields high sensitivity under out-of-distribution noise, this limitation establishes a clear path forward. Future developments will focus on integrating noise-aware adversarial training and explicit domain adaptation to bridge the Sim2Real gap for raw clinical applications.
