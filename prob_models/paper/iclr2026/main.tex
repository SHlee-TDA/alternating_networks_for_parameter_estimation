\begin{abstract}
Estimating the parameters of nonlinear dynamical systems from sparse, partial, and noisy observations is a severely ill-posed inverse problem, and under structural non-identifiability it exposes a failure that is easy to mistake for mere inaccuracy: a network trained to minimize squared error collapses to the conditional mean $\E[\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}]$, which for a non-identifiable ridge is a low-density, often unphysical point rather than a solution. We take this collapse as a starting point, not a conclusion. Since the non-identifiability lives entirely in the unobserved states, the target posterior $p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}})$ is a mixture of sharp, well-posed conditionals $p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \mX_{\mathrm{hid}})$ blurred by hidden-state uncertainty, and the natural sampler for it alternates between a hidden-state conditional and a parameter conditional---the same alternating operator that a deterministic decoupled estimator runs, but whose deterministic fixed point is exactly the collapsed conditional mean. We keep the operator and change what it converges to: replacing each deterministic map with a stochastic kernel turns the iteration into a sampler of the joint posterior. We give two guarantees in this probabilistic regime that parallel the contraction-and-alignment of deterministic work---injected noise makes the chain uniformly ergodic, so a unique invariant law exists with no contraction assumption, and training the two conditionals as denoisers steers that law toward the true posterior with a controlled bound. On sparsely observed physiological and synthetic systems, the framework recovers structured, multimodal posteriors along non-identifiable manifolds without collapse, and we characterize the resulting coverage--sharpness trade-off honestly. Full statements and proofs appear in a companion manuscript.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Inferring the governing parameters of nonlinear dynamical systems from sparse, noisy, and partially observed trajectories is a cornerstone challenge across mathematical biology, ecology, and physiology. The task is fundamentally hard for three compounding reasons: parameter estimation is an ill-posed inverse problem; \emph{sparse} sampling maximizes its ill-conditioning; and \emph{partial} observation induces structural non-identifiability, in which many parameter configurations produce the same observable trajectory.

A natural response is amortized regression: train a network on simulated $(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \vtheta)$ pairs and predict $\vtheta$ directly, in the spirit of simulation-based inference \citep{cranmer2020frontier}. Under structural non-identifiability, however, the squared-error solution is the conditional mean $\E[\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}]$, and for a non-identifiable ridge that mean lies in a low-density---often physically invalid---region between the branches of the solution set. The estimator is then not merely imprecise; it answers the wrong question, reporting a confident point where the data support an extended manifold. The same pathology afflicts \emph{deterministic} decoupled estimators, which split the map $\mX_{\mathrm{obs}} \mapsto \vtheta$ through the hidden state as $\mX_{\mathrm{obs}} \mapsto \mX_{\mathrm{hid}} \mapsto \vtheta$ and iterate two networks to a fixed point: enforcing a global contraction (e.g., via spectral normalization \citep{miyato2018spectral}) secures the fixed point but pins it to the same conditional mean, and the rigidity throttles representational capacity. We treat this collapse (regression-to-the-mean) as the \emph{start} of the problem, not its resolution: it tells us to recover the posterior, not to sharpen a point.

Why is $p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}})$ so poorly behaved, and what fixes it? The non-identifiability is located entirely in the missing states. Conditioning on the \emph{full} trajectory renders $\vtheta$ identifiable, so the conditional $p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \mX_{\mathrm{hid}})$ is sharp and well-posed; the ridge appears only after marginalizing the hidden states,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mixture}
    p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}) = \int p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \mX_{\mathrm{hid}})\, p(\mX_{\mathrm{hid}} \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}})\, d\mX_{\mathrm{hid}} .
\end{equation}
This is the probabilistic rationale for decoupling: separate a well-posed parameter conditional from the hidden-state uncertainty that blurs it, rather than attacking the blurred marginal directly.

The natural sampler for this mixture alternates between the two conditionals---drawing $\mX_{\mathrm{hid}} \sim p(\mX_{\mathrm{hid}} \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \vtheta)$ and then $\vtheta \sim p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \mX_{\mathrm{hid}})$---a (pseudo-)Gibbs sweep defining an operator $\mathcal{T}$. A deterministic decoupled estimator runs exactly this operator with the conditional \emph{means}, so its fixed point is $\E[\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}]$: the collapsed center is not an artifact of a particular architecture but the fixed point of the mean operator, a one-line consequence of iterating $\mathcal{T}$ with means. Our move is to keep the operator and change what it converges to. Replacing each deterministic map by a stochastic kernel lets the same iteration sample the full conditional instead of its mean, so the question shifts from ``does $\mathcal{T}$ have a fixed point'' to ``what law does $\mathcal{T}$ converge to, and is it the right one.'' Deterministic decoupled estimators are thus a baseline class and a limiting case, not a prerequisite. We instantiate the two conditionals as Conditional Variational Autoencoders (a Dual CVAE) and alternate between them with a stochastic Pseudo-Gibbs routine that samples $\mX_{\mathrm{hid}}$ and $\vtheta$ given $\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}$.

Two questions then replace the single fixed-point question, and we answer them in parallel to the contraction-and-alignment guarantees of deterministic work. \emph{Existence}: the injected noise gives $\mathcal{T}$ a strictly positive transition density on a compact state space, making it uniformly ergodic, so a unique invariant law $\pi^\ast$ exists and is reached geometrically---with \emph{no} contraction assumption, in contrast to the spectral-norm contraction a deterministic fixed point requires. \emph{Correctness}: training the two conditionals as denoisers steers $\pi^\ast$ toward the true posterior, the probabilistic counterpart of teacher forcing, with a bound that is controlled rather than zero and a self-consistency certificate computable from the trained model alone. We state these results in abbreviated form and defer full statements, assumptions, and proofs to a companion manuscript \citep{adcvae-theory}.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A \emph{generative decoupling} framework that preserves the physical hidden-state/parameter partition and recasts non-identifiability as structured posterior uncertainty; the two-network alternation is \emph{derived} as a sampler of the joint posterior via the mixture identity~\eqref{eq:mixture}, not adopted as a heuristic.
    \item Two guarantees that replace global spectral contraction with local, noise-driven ones: existence of a unique invariant law by ergodicity (assuming no contraction) and steering of that law toward the true posterior by denoising, accompanied by a computable self-consistency certificate. We state them briefly here and prove them in the companion manuscript \citep{adcvae-theory}.
    \item Empirical evidence, on a physiological model (the OGTT minimal model) and controlled synthetic systems, that the framework recovers structured multimodal posteriors along non-identifiable manifolds without collapse, while single-network and deterministic decoupled baselines collapse to the conditional mean; we characterize the coverage--sharpness trade-off honestly rather than claiming uniform superiority.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background \& Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
Consider a nonlinear dynamical system governed by the ordinary differential equation (ODE) $\dot{\vx}(t) = f(\vx(t); \vtheta)$, where $\vx(t) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ represents the full system states and $\vtheta \in \mathbb{R}^p$ denotes the constant parameters. In realistic scenarios, we only observe a subset of the states $\vx_{\mathrm{obs}} \subset \vx$ at highly sparse time intervals, leaving $\vx_{\mathrm{hid}} = \vx \setminus \vx_{\mathrm{obs}}$ latent.

A \emph{deterministic decoupled} baseline instantiates the two maps as networks $\mX_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(k+1)} = H_\phi(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \vtheta^{(k)})$ and $\vtheta^{(k+1)} = P_\psi(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \mX_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(k+1)})$, defining the sweep operator $\mathcal{T}(\vtheta; \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}) = P_\psi(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, H_\phi(\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \vtheta))$. Enforcing a global Lipschitz constant $\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{T}) < 1$ via spectral normalization \citep{miyato2018spectral} makes $\mathcal{T}$ a contraction with a unique Banach fixed point. Because the two networks are trained to output conditional means, that fixed point is the conditional mean $\E[\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}]$: under structural non-identifiability it lands in the low-density interior of the solution ridge, and the contraction constraint additionally bottlenecks capacity. Our framework retains the operator $\mathcal{T}$ but replaces each deterministic map with a stochastic transition kernel (\Secref{sec:method}), so the same iteration samples the joint posterior instead of contracting to its mean.

\subsection{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Simulation-based inference.}
Amortized posterior estimation trains a conditional density on simulated parameter--data pairs and reuses it across observations \citep{cranmer2020frontier}. Neural posterior estimation and its sequential refinements \citep{papamakarios2016fast, greenberg2019automatic}, benchmarked systematically by \citet{lueckmann2021benchmarking}, typically fit a single conditional $p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}})$ with a normalizing flow \citep{papamakarios2021normalizing}. We differ by \emph{decoupling} through the physical hidden state: rather than model the blurred marginal directly, we sample the sharp conditional $p(\vtheta \mid \mX_{\mathrm{obs}}, \mX_{\mathrm{hid}})$ and marginalize $\mX_{\mathrm{hid}}$ by iteration, injecting ODE structure that a single flow must otherwise learn implicitly.

\paragraph{Score-based generative models.}
That target-noise training recovers the score of the data distribution \citep{vincent2011connection, efron2011tweedie} underpins modern score-based and diffusion models \citep{song2019generative, ho2020denoising, song2021score}, as well as flow-matching and stochastic-interpolant alternatives \citep{lipman2023flow}. Our steering guarantee uses exactly this denoiser-as-score correspondence, but applied to the two learned \emph{conditionals} of the inverse problem rather than to a single unconditional prior.

\paragraph{Score-based posterior sampling and split Gibbs.}
The closest modern neighbors sample a posterior by alternating a data-consistency step with a learned score/denoiser prior: diffusion posterior sampling \citep{chung2023diffusion}, denoiser-driven stochastic solvers \citep{kadkhodaie2021stochastic}, and split-Gibbs / plug-and-play samplers \citep{vono2019split, coeurdoux2024plug}. These target a \emph{known} likelihood paired with a generic prior; our two blocks are instead \emph{two learned conditionals of a partially observed dynamical system}, coupled through the hidden state, and we quantify the bias from their possible incompatibility with a computable certificate.

\paragraph{Compatible conditionals and dependency networks.}
Sampling from independently learned conditionals is pseudo-Gibbs on a dependency network \citep{heckerman2000dependency}, whose stationary law need not correspond to any genuine joint unless the conditionals are compatible \citep{arnold2001conditionally}. Prior treatments are discrete; we extend the construction to a continuous, non-identifiable state space and, instead of assuming compatibility, measure the residual incompatibility and bound its effect (\Secref{subsec:theory}).

\section{Analysis: Expressiveness vs. Robustness Trade-off}
\label{sec:analysis}
To stress-test our model, we evaluate inference degradation by incrementally injecting Gaussian noise into the test observations $\mX_{\mathrm{obs}}$. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:noise}, this experiment uncovers a compelling trade-off between a model's local expressiveness and its vulnerability to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) perturbation.

The high sensitivity of the Dual CVAE originates directly from its superior performance: it maps a razor-thin, precise geometric manifold of the physics equations. When observation noise pushes inputs off-manifold, the generative alignment falters, leading to variance spikes. This reveals that structural precision introduces sensitivity to noise, defining a distinct bias-variance frontier.

\section{Conclusion \& Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a probabilistic decoupled framework for nonlinear inverse problems that keeps the alternating operator of deterministic decoupled estimators but changes what it converges to, replacing global spectral contraction with local, noise-driven guarantees: existence of a unique invariant law by ergodicity and steering toward the true posterior by denoising. By sampling conditionals over the complete physical state space, it recovers structured multimodal posteriors along non-identifiable manifolds where mean-seeking estimators collapse.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our guarantees and experiments target non-identifiability that is \emph{structural}---a low-dimensional fiber induced by missing states. Strong \emph{practical} non-identifiability (near-flat likelihoods without a clean fiber), posteriors with three or more well-separated modes, and observation noise that pushes inputs off the training manifold all stress the method; the noise sensitivity documented in \Secref{sec:analysis} is a direct symptom of the last. The steering guarantee is only as good as the simulator: it bounds the gap to the posterior \emph{under the assumed forward model}, not to reality, so the Sim2Real gap remains open---this is why we frame domain adaptation as future work rather than as a contribution or a demonstrated capability. Finally, the coverage--sharpness trade-off of \Secref{sec:experiments} means our intervals are wide by construction along unidentifiable directions; this width is faithful rather than free, and aggregate interval metrics can undervalue it. Bridging the Sim2Real gap via noise-aware and domain-adaptive training is the natural next step.
