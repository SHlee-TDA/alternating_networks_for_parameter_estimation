\section{Theory 2: Correctness and Statistical Limitations}
\label{sec:correctness}

Theorem~\ref{thm:contraction} establishes that the iteration $\theta^{(k+1)} = \mathcal{T}(\theta^{(k)}; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ converges to a unique fixed point $\theta^*(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ from any initialization, but convergence says nothing about \emph{what} that fixed point is. This section characterizes it, and the conclusion is a characterization rather than a correctness guarantee. We establish three facts. First, because the fixed point is a deterministic function of $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$, it cannot be more accurate than the conditional mean $\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]$ --- the same information-theoretic ceiling faced by a plain direct regressor, which the iterative reconstruction of the hidden state does \emph{not} circumvent (Section~\ref{subsec:ceiling}). Second, the deviation of the fixed point from the conditional mean is controlled by a one-step residual, which we bound and decompose. Third, under non-identifiability the conditional-mean target is itself displaced off the physical solution manifold (Section~\ref{subsec:off_fiber}). The overall picture is that the decoupled fixed-point estimator is a stable, convergent procedure whose output is the (off-fiber) conditional mean, and whose decoupled structure buys no accuracy over direct regression --- a statement we make precise below and confirm empirically in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{The One-Step Residual and the Fixed-Point Error}

To quantify the estimation error at the individual sample level, we define a metric measuring how well the learned operator preserves the true parameter.

\begin{definition}[One-Step Residual]
\label{def:one_step_residual}
For a given observation $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$ and its corresponding ground-truth parameter $\theta^{\mathrm{true}}=\theta^\mathrm{true}(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$, the one-step residual of the iterative operator $\mathcal{T}(\cdot; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ evaluated at the ground truth is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) := \lVert \mathcal{T}(\theta^{\mathrm{true}}; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) - \theta^{\mathrm{true}}\rVert_2.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The one-step residual is a crucial quantity because it directly bounds the distance between the final converged fixed point and the ground-truth parameter.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-Point Error Bound]
\label{lem:error_bound}
Suppose $\mathcal{T}(\cdot; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ is a contraction mapping with a Lipschitz constant $\mathrm{Lip}_\mathcal{T} < 1$, and let $\theta^*=\theta^*(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ be its unique fixed point.
Then, the distance between the fixed point and the ground-truth parameter is strictly bounded by the one-step residual:
\begin{equation}
    \lVert\theta^* - \theta^{\mathrm{true}}\rVert_2 \le \frac{\varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})}{1 - \mathrm{Lip}_\mathcal{T}}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the triangle inequality and the contractivity of $\mathcal{T}(\cdot; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$, we have:
\begin{align*}
    \lVert \theta^* - \theta^{\mathrm{true}}\rVert_2 &= \lVert \mathcal{T}(\theta^\star; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) - \theta^{\mathrm{true}}\rVert_2 \\
    &\le \lVert \mathcal{T}(\theta^*; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) - \mathcal{T}(\theta^{\mathrm{true}}; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})\rVert_2 + \lVert \mathcal{T}(\theta^{\mathrm{true}}; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) - \theta^{\mathrm{true}}\rVert_2 \\
    &\le \mathrm{Lip}_\mathcal{T} \|\theta^* - \theta^{\mathrm{true}}\|_2 + \varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}).
\end{align*}
Rearranging the terms yields the desired bound.
\end{proof}

Teacher forcing \emph{targets} $\varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})=0$: during training, $H_\phi$ and $P_\psi$ are optimized on the true pairs so that $H_\phi(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta^\mathrm{true})\approx \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{hid}$ and $P_\psi(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{hid})\approx \theta^\mathrm{true}$, which would make $\mathcal{T}(\theta^{\mathrm{true}}; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})\approx\theta^{\mathrm{true}}$ and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:error_bound}, pin the fixed point to the ground truth. The remainder of this section shows that this ideal is generally \emph{unreachable}: the one-step residual $\varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ is bounded away from zero, for a structural reason (Section~\ref{subsec:collapse}) and an approximation reason (Remark~\ref{rem:decomposition}), and even in the best case the fixed point can only reach the conditional mean, which we now show is the true ceiling.

\subsection{The Conditional-Mean Ceiling: Reconstructing the Hidden State Adds No Information}
\label{subsec:ceiling}

A natural intuition motivates the decoupled design: since different hidden states and parameters can produce the same observation, a method that reconstructs the hidden state ``ought'' to have an information advantage over a direct regressor $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}\mapsto\theta$ that ignores it. The following proposition shows this intuition is mistaken, because at inference the reconstructed hidden state is itself a function of $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional-mean ceiling]
\label{prop:ceiling}
By uniqueness of the fixed point (Theorem~\ref{thm:contraction}), $\theta^*(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ is a deterministic (measurable) function of $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$. Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the reconstructed hidden state at convergence, $H_\phi\big(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs},\theta^*(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})\big)$, is also a function of $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$, and hence carries no information about $\theta$ beyond what $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$ already contains;
\item[(ii)] the expected squared error is bounded below by the conditional variance,
\[
\mathbb{E}\big\|\theta^*(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})-\theta\big\|_2^2 \;\ge\; \mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{Var}(\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})\big],
\]
which is the same floor obeyed by \emph{any} estimator depending on $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$ alone --- in particular by direct regression --- and is attained only by the conditional mean $\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Uniqueness makes $\theta^*=g(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ for a measurable $g$, giving (i) by composition. For (ii), any measurable $g$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}\|g(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})-\theta\|_2^2 = \mathbb{E}\|\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]-\theta\|_2^2 + \mathbb{E}\|g(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})-\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]\|_2^2 \ge \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})]$, with equality iff $g=\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]$ almost surely.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} identifies the fundamental ceiling. Teacher forcing exploits the \emph{true} hidden state during training, but that advantage does not transfer to inference, where the true hidden state is unavailable and its learned surrogate is a deterministic image of $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$. The decoupled iteration therefore cannot beat the conditional mean, and --- as our experiments show (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}) --- a direct regressor typically \emph{reaches} that ceiling more closely, since it avoids the reconstruction detour that inflates $\varepsilon$. The value of the fixed-point construction thus lies in its convergence guarantee and in the self-consistent hidden trajectory it returns as a by-product, not in improved parameter accuracy.

\subsection{Ambiguity under Non-Identifiability: Regression-to-the-Mean}
\label{subsec:collapse}

The assumption that $\varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ can be universally minimized to zero breaks down when evaluating the model over a statistical data distribution $p(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)$ if the dynamical system exhibits non-identifiability. In such regimes, the inverse mapping is multi-valued, meaning a single observation $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$ can be generated by multiple distinct parameter values.

To formalize the behavior of our neural estimators under this one-to-many mapping, we recall a fundamental result from statistical machine learning theory \cite{hastie2009elements}. 
When a machine learning model is trained using the Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective, it is mathematically constrained in how it handles target ambiguity. 
Rather than selecting an arbitrary valid parameter among the multiple possibilities, the optimal estimator is driven to compute the expected value of the target distribution. 
This well-known property is formally stated as follows.
% 아래 proposition은 machine learning text (Bishop이나 팁쉬라니 등)에 등장하는 theorem임. 이걸 배경지식으로 먼저 던져줘야 함.
\begin{proposition}[Optimal Predictor under Squared-Loss]
\label{prop:optimal_predictor}
Let $(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)$ be random variables representing the observations and parameters drawn from the joint distribution $p(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)$ with $\mathbb{E}[\|\theta\|_2^2] < \infty$. Among all measurable functions $g(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ minimizing the expected risk $\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)}[\|\theta - g(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})\|_2^2]$, the optimal solution is given almost surely by the conditional mean:
\begin{equation}
    g^*(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) = \mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

To understand the consequence of this statistical compromise on our fixed-point framework, we analyze the expected squared one-step residual assuming the network reaches the theoretical limit described in Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_predictor}.

\begin{theorem}[Expected Residual and Conditional Variance]
\label{thm:expected_residual}
Assume the iterative operator perfectly learns the optimal MSE predictor defined in Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_predictor}, such that $\mathcal{T}(\theta; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) = \mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]$ for any input parameter $\theta$. 
Then, the expected squared one-step residual over the joint distribution $p(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)$ is exactly equal to the expected conditional variance of the parameter distribution:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)} \left[ \varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})^2 \right] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}} \left[ \operatorname{Var}(\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) \right].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:one_step_residual} and the assumption that the operator acts as the optimal MSE predictor (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_predictor}), i.e., $\mathcal{T}(\theta^{\mathrm{true}}; \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) = \mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]$, the expected squared residual is:
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)} \left[ \varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})^2 \right] = \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)} \left[ \|\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}] - \theta\|_2^2 \right].
\end{equation*}
Applying the Law of Total Expectation by conditioning on $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}$, we obtain:
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)} \left[ \|\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}] - \theta\|_2^2 \right] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}} \left[ \mathbb{E}_{\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}} \left[ \|\theta - \mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]\|_2^2 \right] \right].
\end{equation*}
The inner expectation is the exact definition of the conditional variance $\operatorname{Var}(\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$. 
Thus, the equality holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Regression-to-the-Mean and Error Lower Bound]
\label{cor:lower_bound}
Under the assumptions of Lemma \ref{lem:error_bound} and Theorem \ref{thm:expected_residual}, the expected squared estimation error of the fixed-point solver is strictly lower-bounded by the structural uncertainty of the system:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)} \left[ \|\theta^*(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) - \theta\|_2^2 \right] \ge \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}} \left[ \operatorname{Var}(\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) \right].
\end{equation}
Furthermore, the upper bound is dictated by the contractivity:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}, \theta)} \left[ \|\theta^*(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) - \theta\|_2^2 \right] \le \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}} \left[ \operatorname{Var}(\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) \right]}{(1 - \mathrm{Lip}_\mathcal{T})^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:lower_bound} mathematically proves that even if the operator perfectly and stably converges ($\mathrm{Lip}_\mathcal{T} < 1$), the fixed point cannot align with the true parameter if $\operatorname{Var}(\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}) > 0$.
Instead, the model structurally collapses to the conditional mean.
This rigorously explains why deterministic iterative solvers applied to non-identifiable inverse problems inevitably suffer from the regression-to-the-mean phenomenon and joint-distribution collapse, highlighting the fundamental limitation of MSE supervision in such regimes.

\subsection{The Conditional Mean Lies Off the Physical Solution Manifold}
\label{subsec:off_fiber}

Corollary~\ref{cor:lower_bound} shows that the estimate collapses to the conditional mean $\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]$. We now sharpen this into a geometric statement: under a multiplicative non-identifiability, the conditional mean is not merely inaccurate --- it is not even a physically admissible solution, because it lies strictly \emph{off} the manifold of parameters consistent with the observation.

For a given observation, write $c=c(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ for the value of the identifiable statistic and let the \emph{fiber} be the set of parameters consistent with it,
\[
\mathcal{F}_c = \{\theta \in \Theta : \theta \text{ reproduces } \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}\},
\]
i.e., the support of the conditional law $p(\theta \mid \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$. The following statement is geometric and does not depend on the specific form of the degeneracy; it says that whenever the fiber is genuinely curved, its conditional mean leaves it.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional mean off a curved fiber]
\label{prop:off_fiber}
Assume the fiber $\mathcal{F}_c\subset\mathbb{R}^p$ is compact and in \emph{strictly convex position}: every point of $\mathcal{F}_c$ is an extreme point of its convex hull $\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{F}_c)$ (equivalently, no point of $\mathcal{F}_c$ is a nontrivial convex combination of other points of $\mathcal{F}_c$). If the conditional law $p(\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$, which is supported on $\mathcal{F}_c$, is non-degenerate (not a single point mass), then
\[
\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]\notin\mathcal{F}_c .
\]
The MSE-optimal estimate (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_predictor}) is therefore not itself a parameter consistent with the observation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $m=\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs}]$ be the barycenter of the conditional law. Since that law is supported on $\mathcal{F}_c\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{F}_c)$, we have $m\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{F}_c)$. By the strictly-convex-position hypothesis, $\mathcal{F}_c$ is exactly the set of extreme points of the compact convex body $\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{F}_c)$. By Bauer's characterization, the barycenter of a probability measure equals an extreme point $e$ if and only if the measure is the Dirac mass $\delta_e$. As the conditional law is non-degenerate, $m$ is not an extreme point of $\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{F}_c)$; hence $m\notin\mathcal{F}_c$.
\end{proof}

The 1-D product degeneracy of the OGTT model is the canonical instance, and there the displacement is fully explicit.

\begin{corollary}[Product degeneracy and its quantitative bias]
\label{cor:off_fiber_product}
Suppose the observation identifies only the product $c=\theta_1\theta_2$ on the physical domain $\theta_1,\theta_2>0$ (the OGTT glucose-only model, where only $mDI=S_I\sigma$ is identifiable, is exactly of this form; see Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}), so that on a bounded parameter box the fiber is the hyperbolic arc $\mathcal{F}_c=\{(\theta_1,c/\theta_1)\}$. Then $\mathcal{F}_c$ is in strictly convex position, so Proposition~\ref{prop:off_fiber} applies; moreover the bias is quantified by
\[
\mathbb{E}[\theta_1\mid c]\,\mathbb{E}[\theta_2\mid c]
= c\,\mathbb{E}[\theta_1\mid c]\,\mathbb{E}[\theta_1^{-1}\mid c]
\;\ge\; c,
\]
with strict inequality unless $\theta_1$ is almost surely constant given $c$. The estimated product thus exceeds the true identifiable value $c$: the conditional mean lies strictly on the convex side of the fiber.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The map $\theta_1\mapsto c/\theta_1$ is strictly convex on $(0,\infty)$, so the hyperbolic arc lies strictly on one side of each of its chords; every point is therefore extreme in $\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{F}_c)$, giving strictly convex position. For the bound, $\theta_2=c/\theta_1$ on $\mathcal{F}_c$ gives $\mathbb{E}[\theta_2\mid c]=c\,\mathbb{E}[\theta_1^{-1}\mid c]$, and Jensen's inequality for the strictly convex $x\mapsto x^{-1}$ yields $\mathbb{E}[\theta_1^{-1}\mid c]\ge(\mathbb{E}[\theta_1\mid c])^{-1}$, with equality iff $\theta_1$ is a.s.\ constant. Multiplying by $\mathbb{E}[\theta_1\mid c]>0$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:off_fiber} shows the failure is \emph{structural and statistical}, not an artifact of the solver: any estimator driven to the conditional mean by a squared-loss objective (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_predictor}) is pushed off the physical solution manifold whenever the fiber is curved and the fiber-conditional law is non-degenerate. Corollary~\ref{cor:off_fiber_product} additionally gives a directly checkable signature in the product case --- the recovered product $\widehat\theta_1\widehat\theta_2$ is biased away from the true identifiable statistic $c$ --- which we examine empirically in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\begin{remark}[Two sources of the fixed-point error]
\label{rem:decomposition}
The one-step residual $\varepsilon(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})$ governing the bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:error_bound} conflates two distinct contributions:
(i) a \emph{structural} part, whose expected square equals the conditional variance $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})]$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:expected_residual}) and is irreducible under non-identifiability; and
(ii) an \emph{operator-approximation} part that persists even when the observation is fully identifying ($\operatorname{Var}(\theta\mid\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{obs})=0$), reflecting the finite capacity and teacher-forced training of the learned composition $P_\psi\!\circ\! H_\phi$.
The two are separable experimentally: a structurally identifiable system exposes (ii) in isolation, whereas a non-identifiable system is dominated by (i). Our experiments in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} instantiate both regimes.
\end{remark}
